Write a Python module and save it as calc.py for a tiny language of arithmetic on whole numbers. An expression has numbers (0 or more), the operators +, -, * and @, round brackets, and maybe spaces. a @ b is the smaller of a and b. * binds more tightly than + and -, as usual; @ binds more tightly than *. +, - and * group from the left; @ groups from the left (a @ b @ c is (a @ b) @ c). Brackets are worked out first. The module has two functions. 1) tokens(text) returns the list of the expression's pieces as strings, spaces left out (tokens('12 *(3@4)') is ['12', '*', '(', '3', '@', '4', ')']). 2) evaluate(text) returns the expression's value as an int. Test both yourself before you finish. Then tell me the value of evaluate('13 @ 10').
