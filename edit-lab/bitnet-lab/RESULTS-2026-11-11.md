# BitNet direct-training experiments — results, 2026-11-11

Question (Arnav): Fable's vote counter trains BitNets directly. Can it be improved — and does
stacking two ternary weights per weight ("more layered weights") get closer to full precision?

## Setup (read before the numbers)

- **Reimplementation, not their code.** `train.py` here reimplements the mechanisms from the
  study of Fable's `scratch.Direct`. Fable's repo was never executed or modified.
- **Own corpus.** Fable's harvested corpus is not in their repo, so this lab trained on its own:
  5.92M BPE pieces (their shared tokenizer) from 393 unique prose files in the local workspace.
  **Absolute losses are therefore NOT comparable to Fable's 4.141 / 4.031.** What transfers is
  the arm-vs-arm comparison under matched conditions, which is what every verdict below uses.
- **Model:** their stem shape — 4 blocks, dim 192, vocab 4096, context 256; 2,615,424 weights,
  1,769,472 of them ternary (layered_half: 2 blocks, 884,736 ternary).
- **Judge:** held loss on a fixed set of 128 held windows (every-40th-window rule; no arm ever
  trained on them), measured after the run at full rounding. Same seed → same init and same
  batch stream across arms. 8-minute runs, 3 seeds each, batch 32, OMP/MKL pinned to 2 threads.
- Vote arms' shared parts train exactly as Fable's Direct (AdamW lr 1e-3, betas 0.9/0.95, no
  weight decay; per-layer scale by the hand-set gradient). The STE arm uses Fable's built-in
  recipe (lr 3e-3, warmup 200, rounding eased in over the first half) — their strongest honest
  published-way settings. (Their original 8-minute published-way run's exact settings are not
  recorded — study limit #2 — so the tuned recipe is the fair target.)

## Verdicts

**1. Reproduction — the mechanism checks out.** Plain-32 votes learn (init 8.37 → held 3.074)
and STE beats them by 0.079 at equal minutes — the same direction and rough size as Fable's
own table (their train-loss gap: 0.110). The vote counter at 1.25 B/w of state reaches within
0.08 of a trainer using 12 B/w. Baseline on this machine is now trustworthy.

**2. Magnitude-weighted votes — LOSS.** 3.135 vs plain's 3.074: worse than plain by 0.061, in
the opposite direction from its bar (beat plain by >2 sd and >0.01, and close at least half
the distance to STE). Diagnosis in the record: its flip rate was a third of plain's
(0.00011 vs 0.00037 of weights per step) — with clipped increments mostly 0 or ±1, the ±96
threshold made the arm effectively far colder than intended. As specified, it lost. A fair
retest would first calibrate the threshold by the measured mean |increment|; that is a
different arm, not this result.

**3. Layered weights (Arnav's idea) — split answer, read carefully.**
- *At equal weight count:* layered 3.098 vs plain 3.074 — **no gain** (0.023 worse, inside
  plain's seed noise), at **double** the state bytes. It did not close the distance to STE
  (still 0.10 behind). In 8 minutes the ±2 levels were barely populated (1.7% of weights at
  each of −2/+2) — the second ternary layer had only begun to be used, so this is a verdict
  about 8 minutes, not about the idea's ceiling. A founding-length (40 min) run is the honest
  next test.
- *At equal state bytes* (half the weights, 2 blocks): layered_half posted the best held loss
  of the day, 2.899 — ahead of STE by 0.096. **But the confound is structural:** the smaller
  model took ~37% more steps in the same 8 minutes, and its ordinary-trained share (embeddings
  and norms, trained by AdamW in every arm) is proportionally twice as large — so more of its
  learning rode on ordinary gradients, not on votes. This is an efficiency result (more steps
  per minute at the same state budget), **not** evidence that stacked ternary weights learn
  better per step. No equal-steps control was run; claiming one from this data would be wrong.

**4. Threshold annealing 8→64 — LOSS, decisively.** 3.850, worse than plain by 0.78. Starting
at 8 votes fires flips on weak early evidence (its flip rate ran ~2× plain's and the loss got
worse, not better); raising the threshold later cannot undo the early disorganization. The
vote-side analogue of Fable's lr cool-down does not transfer at this scale and budget.

## Incidents (full audit trail in `sweep.log`)

- The first plain seed-0 run shared the machine with the composites job's second half (the
  sweep started in the gap between its two runs): 52 steps vs 121–124 clean. Discarded and
  re-run; the record is preserved in `runs-discarded.jsonl` with the reason.
- Two runtime restarts killed the sweep mid-run (STE seed 0, then anneal seed 0). Both were
  re-run cleanly. Final dataset is complete: 18/18 runs, 3 seeds per arm, no contention
  signatures in any kept run.

## What I would run next (not run tonight)

1. Magnitude votes with the threshold calibrated by measured mean |increment| — the idea
   deserves one fairly-set run before it is buried.
2. Layered at founding length (40 min) — the ±2 levels need time to populate before the idea
   can be judged.
3. An equal-steps control for layered_half, to separate "trains faster per minute" from
   "learns better per step."
