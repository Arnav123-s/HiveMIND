### EN-001 ★ · Error-anatomy → targeted-data loop, closed in both directions
- **programs coupled:** BitNet mill (error anatomy, ER-001..ER-012 / CANDIDATES-J) × factory/hive (dream generators, `factory/dream_gen.py`, `dream_gen_niches.py`) × mill trainer
- **mechanism:** The anatomy runner (`held_loss_anatomy()`, per-target rows: window, position, target, loss + provenance sidecar) publishes its worst slices — by source class, frequency band, position bucket — as a machine-readable weakness manifest. The factory's execution-verified generators, which already manufacture proved-label data at corpus scale (628-item dream pools), generate a targeted training slice aimed at exactly those slices (e.g. prose windows carrying ≥55% of loss mass at ~3.4–3.7, not the junk-diluted pooled 2.8580). The mill trains ONE arm on champion data + targeted slice and re-runs the SAME anatomy cut. Reverse leg: mill arms that win on a slice donate their per-target anatomy rows back to the factory as a difficulty map for its own dream-pool filtering.
- **interface:** `mill/anatomy/weakness-manifest.json` (slice id, target share, mean loss, loss-mass share, champion checkpoint sha) → factory generator config; return leg `factory/reports/targeted-slice-receipt.json` (items generated, contamination-filter survivors, generator family) → mill arm definition in `mill/screen.py`. Anatomy assert discipline carries over: if the provenance stream cannot be re-derived byte-for-byte, the manifest voids itself.
- **payoff:** Converts the lab's best diagnosis (what 2.8580 is *made of*) into the factory's best capability (proved data at scale) instead of leaving anatomy as a report and dream pools as untargeted supply. Predicted: the first arm whose training data is chosen by measured loss mass rather than by hypothesis.
- **coupling risk:** If the coupling is wrong — anatomy slices are v1-held artefacts (duplicate leak, J-037; junk dilution, ER-001) — the factory manufactures excellent data for a rigged target and the mill "improves" the leak. Mitigation is in the mechanism: manifest carries the corpus version, and no targeted arm is judged until E-018/K-001 re-baselining denominates the slice in v2/v3 held.
- **status:** queued

### EN-002 · PRIORITIES attack order → compute-department scheduler policy
- **programs coupled:** mill (PRIORITIES.md top-40, cost classes) × compute department (`bitnet-lab/compute/`, `mill/CONCURRENCY.md`, ladder/growth preemption rules)
- **mechanism:** The scheduler stops being first-come-first-served with two preemptors. It reads the attack order as its priority table: mill-changes and eval-only entries (PRIORITIES ranks 1–4, 6–8: G-037 futility stop, G-038 probe ladder, G-026/WH-051 readouts) preempt ordinary probes; full-trial-only entries (E-018, G-049, G-050) are batched into the gaps the ladder leaves, never started if they would straddle the ~22:50 PT growth window (the ladder driver's existing rule, generalised). When `bench_concurrency.py` finally runs, its verdict (`mill/concurrency.json`) sets worker count *per cost class*: sweep probes may run concurrent first, trials stay at CONCURRENCY=1 until their honesty check exists.
- **interface:** `mill/priorities-schedule.json` — a derived, dated extract of PRIORITIES.md (rank, id, cost class, est. minutes) consumed by `compute/queue/` and `mill/run-forever.sh`; PRIORITIES.md remains the human source, the JSON is regenerated on merge, never hand-edited.
- **payoff:** The top-40's "under 3 hours to first verdicts" claim is currently a hand calculation; this makes it the scheduler's actual behaviour, and stops a 24-min trial from blocking a 75-second readout that would have killed three other entries.
- **coupling risk:** If the extract goes stale against PRIORITIES.md, the scheduler faithfully executes a superseded order — worse than no order, because it looks principled. Kill condition: any rank mismatch between JSON and PRIORITIES.md at queue-build time halts scheduling to manual, it does not guess.
- **status:** queued

### EN-003 ★ · Mill verdicts → factory growth gates (one verdict format, two consumers)
- **programs coupled:** BitNet mill (PROMOTE.md adoption guard: >2 sd AND >0.01, ≥2-of-3 seeds) × factory/hive (`factory/growth.py attempt_birth()`, `growth_cycle.py`, H-003 shadow gate)
- **mechanism:** Factory births currently pass pristine dual slice gates and still regressed serving twice (cycles 6/7: 0.941→0.934, 0.941→0.9375) because the factory's gates and the mill's guard are different logics. Adopt the mill's guard verbatim as the factory's *third* gate, after H-003's remainder floor + shadow simulation: a child is adopted only if its served-path delta also clears a mill-style margin (>2× the battery's replicate sd AND >0.01 overall, ≥2-of-3 battery seeds/runs where applicable). Conversely, the mill imports H-003's lesson: a probe/trial win that regresses a *remainder* slice (the equivalent of the factory's per-class scorecard, HV-007) is logged as a near-band entry, not a clean promote.
- **interface:** Shared verdict record `verdict.jsonl` schema (entry id, before/after per-slice scores, sd source, guard outcome) written by both `mill/screen.py` and `factory/growth_cycle.py write_report()`; `factory/audit.py full_audit()` and the mill ledger both read it. No shared code — a shared *record format* only.
- **payoff:** One adoption language across the sprint: a "win" means the same thing in the trainer and the hive, and the exact failure that cost two repair cycles (gate-passing births destroying serving value) becomes structurally unrepeatable in either program.
- **coupling risk:** If the coupling is wrong — battery sd and held-loss sd are not commensurable noise floors — the imported guard is either a rubber stamp or a permanent veto on factory growth. Mitigation: the guard is calibrated on the factory's own replicate spread first (HV-004's pinned BATTERY_SECTIONS definition is a precondition), and a cycle that passes the shadow gate yet fails the mill guard is reported as a calibration finding, not silently blocked forever.
- **status:** queued

### EN-004 ★ · Ladder rungs auto-donate checkpoints as MC-family composition donors
- **programs coupled:** scale ladder (`run_scale_ladder.py`: 2M×3 seeds, 10M×2+1, 50M×1+1, plain+rope ride-along, 0.9M calibration) × mill model-composition family (G-049 shared-embedding pair, G-050 counter-sum merge, G-053 majority soup, G-054 counter transplant, grafts) × mill trainer (`--save-state`, `--counter-merge`)
- **mechanism:** Every ladder job already trains exactly the donor configurations the MC family otherwise pays full price to manufacture: same recipe, multiple seeds, a plain-vs-layered contrast at 2M, a calibration rung. On job completion the ladder driver saves full trainer state (t, c, log_scales, FP state) under the MC donor contract and registers the checkpoint in a donor index — no extra training runs. G-050/G-053's paired experiment, G-049's pair, and the graft arms then draw donors from the index; only shape-mismatched compositions (donors must share shape within a rung) pay for bespoke runs.
- **interface:** `bitnet-lab/ladder-donors.json` (rung, arm, seed, state-file path, sha256, served bytes, solo held) written by `run_scale_ladder.py` on completion; mill merge paths (`--counter-merge`, `--state-merge`, `--ens-eval`) read donors only from this index. The 50M rung donates only if its `--ckpt` state is complete — a partial state is recorded as non-donor, never silently padded.
- **payoff:** G-050 is PRIORITIES #34 and G-049 #33, both full-trial-only largely *because* donors cost runs. The ladder has already spent those runs for a different question (loss-per-byte). Donation cuts the MC family's marginal cost to the merge/eval passes alone and gives the counter-merge question donors at three scales instead of one.
- **coupling risk:** If the coupling is wrong — ladder donors were trained at ladder step budget (56 steps) for a per-byte comparison, not to trial format — merges built on them answer "do counters merge at ladder budget?", a different and weaker claim than G-050's filed prediction, and a win could be over-read. Mitigation: donor index records step budget per checkpoint; any MC verdict drawn from ladder donors is labelled ladder-budget until one trial-format donor pair confirms it.
- **status:** queued

### EN-005 · Kernel-stream verdicts → trainer and serving defaults, through one bench ledger
- **programs coupled:** kernel streams (`kernels/` matmul/attention/vote benches, `kernels-attn/`, `kernels-lut/` RESULTS-LUT) × BitNet trainer (`train.py`) × factory serving path (`factory/serve.py`)
- **mechanism:** Today each kernel stream keeps its own results file and neither consumer is obliged to read any of them. One bench ledger becomes the only route by which a kernel result changes a default: a kernel (ternary-by-int8 matmul, LUT, attention variant) is recorded with (shape, rung size, CPU threads, speedup, numeric-delta vs the plain kernel on a fixed probe batch). The trainer adopts it for a rung only if its numeric delta is inside the mill's honesty bound (the CONCURRENCY.md rule generalised: a speed win that shifts held loss beyond replicate spread is not a win); the factory adopts it for serving only at shapes the hive actually serves. Fable's side's BitNet runtime fault (perplexity ~70, incoherent samples, suspected parallel kernels) is the standing warning this protocol exists to heed — on Muse's side only, as a lesson, never touching Fable's side.
- **interface:** `bitnet-lab/kernels/bench-ledger.jsonl` (one row per kernel×shape verdict, incl. the probe-batch numeric delta) consumed by `train.py` kernel selection flags and `factory/serve.py` load path; each consumer logs which ledger row it adopted.
- **payoff:** Kernel work stops being three parallel diaries and becomes measured supply to the two programs that feel step time: ladder rungs (50M at ~85 min/run) and mill throughput. It also creates the paper trail that distinguishes a fast kernel from a wrong one before a wrong one contaminates a verdict.
- **coupling risk:** If the numeric-delta probe batch is unrepresentative, a kernel passes the ledger and then shifts results only at 50M shapes — the most expensive place to discover it. Mitigation: ledger rows are per-shape and expire at shapes they were not measured on; adoption at a new shape requires its own row.
- **status:** queued

### EN-006 · Factory spawn-mining → mill pool governance (failure shapes rank both queues)
- **programs coupled:** factory/hive (`factory/spawn_mining.py`, H-009 mined growth targets, H-014 daemon mining) × mill pool governance (K-037 family budgets, K-038 premise graph, PRIORITIES defer list)
- **mechanism:** Spawn mining already ranks the hive's failure shapes from the serve log (waking overrule 14/37, fallback-unverified 6/37, swapped-operands raw recall 0.0). The same ranking discipline — *measured failure mass decides what gets worked on next* — is applied to the mill pool: each pass, the mill computes its own failure-mass table (families with repeated near-band deaths, premises gating the most deferred entries per K-038, slices of held where anatomy loss mass concentrates) and K-037's family budgets are allocated by that table instead of uniformly. In return, factory growth targets drawn from mining (H-009) must cite the mill-style premise check: a target whose premise entry is deferred in PRIORITIES (e.g. anything gated on F-043's V-scaling answer) is enqueued as blocked, not as work.
- **interface:** `factory/spawn_mining.py build_report()` output shape becomes the template for `mill/failure-mass.json` (shape, mass, source counts, gating premises); both queues (`factory/scheduler.py` queue, mill pass order) read their own file in the same schema.
- **payoff:** Two programs independently invented "let measured failures rank the work" (H-009/H-014 factory-side; K-037/K-038 mill-side). Sharing the schema means a failure shape discovered in serving can be checked against the trainer pool in one lookup — e.g. whether any pooled arm already attacks it — instead of being re-mined from scratch.
- **coupling risk:** If the coupling is wrong — serve-log failure mass (37-call samples) is far noisier than mill pass statistics — importing its ranking style imports its noise tolerance too, and small-sample shapes start steering a 638-entry pool. Mitigation: the schema carries raw counts, and the mill side requires its own minimum mass before a shape can move a family budget; the factory's thresholds are not inherited.
- **status:** queued

### EN-007 · Compute-department capacity truth → ladder and mill promises (one capacity file)
- **programs coupled:** compute department (`bitnet-lab/compute/CAPACITY.md`, `COMPUTE-ASHP.md`, probe scripts) × scale ladder × mill × factory growth cycles (the ~6h hive-growth cron)
- **mechanism:** Each program currently carries its own implicit machine model: the ladder estimates its own job times, the mill prices a pass at ~7.7h, CONCURRENCY.md records CONCURRENCY=1 as an interim honesty setting, growth cycles preempt on a clock window. One capacity file, owned by the compute department and measured (startup probes, phase probes, the concurrency study when it runs), states: usable CPU/memory under the 1.5 vCPU / 6 GB cap, per-shape step-time table, preemption windows, and the current concurrency verdict. Ladder ETAs, mill pass pricing in PRIORITIES-style arithmetic, and growth-cycle scheduling all read it; none keeps a private estimate.
- **interface:** `bitnet-lab/compute/capacity.json` (versioned, dated, with the measuring script named per figure) read by `run_scale_ladder.py`, `mill/screen.py` pass pricing, and `factory/growth_cycle.py` window checks.
- **payoff:** Kills the class of error where three programs each plan as if they own the machine — the reason the concurrency study is still PENDING (the ladder owned the lab at build time) and the reason growth rollbacks and ladder runs have collided before. Scheduling arguments become arithmetic on shared numbers.
- **coupling risk:** If the capacity file is wrong (measured under conditions that don't hold during a 50M run — thermal, neighbour load on shared vCPUs), every consumer is wrong together, correlated instead of independently. Mitigation: capacity rows carry their measurement conditions, and consumers must fall back to solo/conservative settings when a run's observed step time deviates >25% from the table — deviation itself is logged back to the compute department as a re-measure trigger.
- **status:** queued

### EN-008 · Hive battery ⇄ mill held set: cross-judging each other's champions
- **programs coupled:** factory/hive (serving battery 0.941, sealed bug slice, H-013 sealed v3, HV-005 fresh parallel forms) × BitNet mill (held set, J-037 dosed-leak calibration, E-018/K-001 re-baselining)
- **mechanism:** Both programs judge themselves on sets they also select against — the mill's v1 held is duplicate-leaked (J-037: a leak ≥ rotary's whole 0.0411 margin is plausible); the factory's sealed v2 has been measured 11+ times and gates its own replacements (H-013). Cross-judge: (a) the mill's champion is scored, eval-only, on a fresh factory-style parallel form (new surface families, contamination-filtered against train ∪ held) — a number nobody selected against; (b) the factory's serving composition is scored on a newly sealed slice built with the mill's dosed-leak calibration applied, so its flattery is priced, not assumed zero. Each program keeps its own gate; the cross numbers are reported beside the home numbers and divergence is itself the published measurement of absorbed selection pressure.
- **interface:** Exchange format = each side's existing held/battery loader contract (`factory/bug_heldout_v2.py` pattern; mill held windows file) plus a one-page cross-judge receipt (set sha, contamination filter inputs, home score, cross score) filed in both `mill/` and `factory/reports/`.
- **payoff:** The sprint's two headline numbers (2.8580, 0.941) are both, independently, suspected of flattering their owners. One cross-judge pass prices both suspicions with machinery each side already trusts — because it is the *other* side's machinery.
- **coupling risk:** If the coupling is wrong — the two sets measure genuinely different competences (language modelling vs code-defect judgement) — a low cross score is meaningless and could be misread as dishonesty in the home number. Mitigation is definitional: cross-judging applies only within competence (fresh LM held for the champion; fresh code slices for the composition), never LM-on-code or code-on-LM, and the receipt states the competence it prices.
- **status:** queued

### EN-009 · Dream-pool supply line: factory generators feed mill data-construction arms
- **programs coupled:** factory/hive (`factory/dream.py run_dream_subject()`, verified pools, contamination + difficulty filters) × mill data-construction entries (G-014 sequential stream walk, G-013–G-024 corpus-v2 family, K-wave corpus-v3 fixes FD-004/005/006)
- **mechanism:** The mill's data arms currently each construct their own stream variants inside trainer flags. The factory's dream machinery is the lab's only production-grade verified-data pipeline (execution-proved labels, contamination filtering that cut 628→172 to keep purity). Route mill data construction through it where the arm needs *constructed* data: corpus-v3's document-level decontamination (FD-004) and split hygiene (FD-006) run as factory-style filter passes with survivor counts reported, and G-014's stream-walk ordering is built as a named supply file the trainer consumes, rather than a sampler flag whose output nobody can inspect.
- **interface:** Supply files under `bitnet-lab/mill/supply/` (stream file + `supply-receipt.json`: generator/filter chain, input count, survivor count, contamination inputs) consumed by `train.py --corpus` resolution — which also fixes K-001's verified plumbing bug (`--corpus v3` not resolving to corpus-v3.u16) by making resolution go through the receipt, not a filename guess.
- **payoff:** Data defects in v3 were found by *reading* (FD-004/005/006), i.e. by luck and diligence. A supply-line protocol makes every future corpus pass through the filters that caught them, with counts — and the mill gains the factory's verified-data discipline without rebuilding it.
- **coupling risk:** If the coupling is wrong — dream-pool filtering is tuned for short code items, not LM streams — its contamination filter could strip legitimate repeated prose (formulaic English) and silently bias the corpus toward the unusual. Mitigation: filter passes on LM data report per-source survivor rates (the anatomy sidecar classes); a source class losing >2× the median rate halts the supply build for human review.
- **status:** queued

### EN-010 · Waking-dream verification ⇄ vote-trainer readout: execution checks at both ends
- **programs coupled:** factory/hive (waking-dream layer: composer proposes, execution disposes; H-001 say fitted on path outcomes; HV-001 contract probe) × BitNet mill readout family (G-026 counter-resolved readout, G-030 counter-soft install, WH-051 frustration census, G-027/G-036 logit ensembles)
- **mechanism:** Both programs discovered the same pattern independently: a cheap proposer plus a verifier that reads *unresolved state* beats the proposer alone — the waking layer reads adjusted margins and verifies by execution; G-026 reads the counters the install step discards. Couple them as one design rule with a shared test: any readout/verification layer must be fitted on *downstream path outcomes*, not its proposer's raw scores (H-001's exact diagnosis of why the learned say lost clean recall 0.8182→0.6364). Concretely: G-026/G-030 readouts are evaluated not on held alone but on held *after* the G-027 ensemble readout consumes them, and the factory's next say fit reuses the mill's counter-histogram forecast (WH-049: predict final outcome from mid-run state, target R² ≥ 0.7) as its early-stopping signal for fit iterations.
- **interface:** No shared code; a shared evaluation protocol written once in `mill/.idea-engine/` + `factory/reports/` cross-reference: readout entries file a `path-outcome` section (proposer-only, verifier-only, full-path scores) in the same three-line format in both programs' reports.
- **payoff:** Prevents the same objective-mismatch bug (fitting the proposer, judging the path) from being paid for a third time in a third subsystem, and gives the mill's readout cluster — PRIORITIES #3, #11, #23, #40, four of the top 40 — a single coherent judging frame instead of four separate eval passes.
- **coupling risk:** If the coupling is wrong — the analogy is superficial (execution verification is *proof*; counter resolution is *inference*) — forcing one protocol could make the mill over-trust counter readouts by dressing them in the waking layer's proved-label authority. Mitigation: the protocol's three-line format keeps the verifier's epistemic class explicit per entry (proved / measured / predicted); a readout may not borrow the word "verified."
- **status:** queued

### EN-011 · Shared rollback & byte-vault discipline: one recovery protocol for mill, ladder, factory
- **programs coupled:** factory/hive (HV-006 byte vault + lineage replay, cycle-6/7 repair records, `growth_cycle.py _snapshot_model_files()`) × scale ladder × BitNet mill (champion checkpoints, donor states)
- **mechanism:** The factory learned the hardest lesson in the sprint twice: rollback deleted serving files instead of restoring them, and "no surviving copy of either byte string exists on this VM" forced rebuilds (repairs 006/007; the snapshot fix covered only one cycle path, then cycle 7 found another). The mill and ladder hold equally irreplaceable bytes — champion seed checkpoints, the 50M rung's state, JEPA trunks (F-031's premise is that they still exist on disk) — with no vault at all. Extend HV-006's adoption helper sprint-wide: before any program overwrites a checkpoint/state file it lists as load-bearing, prior bytes go to the content-addressed vault (sha256-named) with a lineage event; the ladder driver and mill screen adopt through the same helper the factory paths use.
- **interface:** One vault root `~/workspace/edit-lab/vault/` (sha256 filename, program tag in the lineage event) + `lineage.jsonl` event schema shared by `factory/registry.py`, `run_scale_ladder.py`, and `mill/screen.py`. Replay/restore drill (HV-009 pattern) run once per program on a cloned lab before the protocol is declared live.
- **payoff:** Converts a factory-only, twice-paid repair into sprint-wide insurance at near-zero marginal cost — the vault helper is already designed (HV-006); the coupling is adoption, not invention. Protects EN-004's donor index and F-031's JEPA-initializer premise, both of which silently assume old bytes still exist.
- **coupling risk:** If the coupling is wrong — vaulting everything load-bearing on a 6 GB-capped VM with 625 MB-class pushes in its history — the vault itself becomes the disk-pressure failure that kills a 50M run mid-flight. Mitigation: vault quotas per program with oldest-first expiry for non-champion bytes, and the ladder's free-space preflight reads vault size as part of its refusal check.
- **status:** queued

### EN-012 · One sprint clock: growth windows, ladder runs, and mill passes on a single calendar protocol
- **programs coupled:** factory growth cycles (~6h cron, ~22:50 PT window the ladder already avoids) × scale ladder (job ETAs up to ~115 min at 50M) × mill (run-forever passes, concurrency study still PENDING for want of a free machine) × compute department
- **mechanism:** Replace pairwise courtesy rules (ladder avoids growth window; concurrency bench refuses to start while anything owns the machine; mill preempted by both) with one published sprint calendar: recurring windows are declared (growth, ladder-priority, mill-pass, and a standing weekly *unowned* window reserved for deferred machinery studies — the concurrency bench gets the first one), and every long job files its window claim with start/end estimates from EN-007's capacity file. A job that overruns its claim yields at its next checkpoint rather than straddling the next window — the ladder already checkpoints at 50M (`--ckpt`); the mill's pass loop gains the same yield point between probes.
- **interface:** `bitnet-lab/compute/sprint-calendar.json` (window list, claims, owning program) read by `run_scale_ladder.py busy()`, `mill/bench_concurrency.py` self-guard, `mill/run-forever.sh`, and `factory/growth_cycle.py`; claims are appended by the jobs themselves, conflicts resolve by the EN-002 priority table.
- **payoff:** The concurrency study — which decides whether every future mill pass costs 7.7h or materially less — has been blocked since build time purely by the absence of an unowned window. A calendar is the cheapest mechanism in this file and it unblocks the single largest throughput unknown in the sprint.
- **coupling risk:** If the coupling is wrong — windows fitted to today's job mix fossilise — a future urgent run (a repair, a re-baseline after J-037 lands) finds every hour owned and the calendar becomes the bureaucracy it replaced. Mitigation: any program may claim the unowned window by filing the reason in the calendar itself, and repair-class work outranks all standing windows by definition, stated in the file.
- **status:** queued

## Closing note — highest payoff per unit of coordination cost

**EN-004 (ladder checkpoints auto-donate as MC donors).** Every other high-payoff coupling
requires two programs to change behaviour and keep a shared artefact honest forever
(EN-001's manifest, EN-003's verdict format, EN-002's schedule extract). EN-004 requires
exactly one change in one place — the ladder driver saving state it already produces, in
the format the mill's merge paths already consume — and it converts sunk compute (the
ladder's multi-seed rungs) into the donor supply whose absence is the stated reason two
top-40 entries (G-049 #33, G-050 #34) cost full trials. Its coordination cost is a file
format; its payoff is a founding-mechanism question ("do counters merge better than
weights?") answered at three scales for the price of eval passes. EN-001 may pay more in
absolute terms; per unit of coordination, nothing else in this wave is close.

*End of wave-7 lens-1 synthesis. Entries: 12 (EN-001..EN-012), all status queued.
★ top 3: EN-001, EN-003, EN-004.*
